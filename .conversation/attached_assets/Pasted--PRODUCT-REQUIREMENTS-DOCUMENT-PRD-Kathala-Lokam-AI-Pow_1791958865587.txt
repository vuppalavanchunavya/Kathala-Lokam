# PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (PRD)
# Kathala Lokam — AI-Powered Indian Children's Storytelling Platform

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Product Type:** Responsive Web Application  
**Target Platform:** Web, Mobile, and Tablet  
**Development Platform:** Replit  
**Primary Audience:** Children aged 3–12 years and their parents  
**Product Category:** Children's Entertainment, Education, and Cultural Storytelling

---

# 1. Product Overview

## 1.1 Product Name

**Kathala Lokam**

The name represents a world of stories, imagination, and childhood memories.

## 1.2 Product Vision

Kathala Lokam is a child-friendly digital storytelling platform designed to preserve traditional Indian stories and make them enjoyable for today's generation of children.

In the past, grandparents told children stories such as Akbar and Birbal, Pedarasi Peddamma Kathalu, Vikram and Betal, Panchatantra, and Tenali Raman. These stories were also popular through books and television programmes.

Kathala Lokam aims to bring these stories back to children through a beautiful digital platform featuring ready-made stories, cute illustrations, expressive narration, multiple Indian languages, and an engaging listening experience.

The application should recreate the feeling of sitting beside a grandparent and listening to an interesting story, while using modern technology to make the experience more accessible.

## 1.3 Problem Statement

Children increasingly consume digital entertainment, but traditional Indian folk tales are not always easily discoverable in a single, child-friendly application with consistent narration and language support.

Parents may also find it difficult to identify suitable, age-appropriate storytelling content in their preferred Indian languages.

Kathala Lokam addresses these problems by providing a central library of curated Indian stories that children can browse and listen to independently, with parental guidance where appropriate.

## 1.4 Product Goals

1. Preserve and promote traditional Indian childhood stories.
2. Make stories easy for children to discover and listen to.
3. Provide cute, expressive, natural-sounding narration.
4. Support Telugu, English, and Hindi initially.
5. Make storytelling enjoyable through colourful illustrations and optional animated videos.
6. Provide a safe, age-appropriate experience.
7. Allow authorised administrators to manage the entire story library.
8. Make content reusable across languages and presentation formats.
9. Provide a foundation for future expansion to additional Indian languages and stories.

## 1.5 Core Product Principle

**Children are story listeners, not story creators.**

The application must contain ready-made stories that have already been added, reviewed, and published by an administrator.

Children must be able to:

- Open the app.
- Browse story categories.
- Select a story.
- Choose an available language.
- Listen to narration.
- View illustrations or an available animated video.
- Read the story if they prefer.
- Save favourite stories.
- Continue listening or select another story.

Do not expose story-writing prompts, AI story generators, video-creation forms, or technical configuration options in the children's interface.

The application should feel like entering a magical Indian children's storybook.

It must not look like a corporate website, a generic educational portal, or a developer dashboard.

Visual inspiration should come from:
- Illustrated children's picture books.
- Colourful Indian folk tales.
- Magical story kingdoms.
- Cute animated animals.
- Friendly cartoon characters.
- Playful children's entertainment applications.

The design should feel polished, consistent, welcoming, and delightful.

- A magical open storybook.
- A sparkling star.
- A friendly moon or cloud.

- Large story-cover illustrations.

- Gentle transitions.

# 10. Expressive Story Narration

## 10.1 Narration Goals

Narration should sound warm, friendly, expressive, and engaging.

The desired experience is similar to a grandparent telling a story to a child.

Avoid monotone, overly fast, robotic narration.

The narrator should convey:
- Curiosity.
- Excitement.
- Surprise.
- Humour.
- Gentle suspense.
- Warmth.
- Calmness for bedtime stories.

## 10.2 Character Voices

Where supported by the chosen narration provider, use distinct, appropriate voices for different characters.

For example:
- King Akbar: warm, dignified, and friendly.
- Birbal: clever, playful, and confident.
- A narrator: expressive and easy to understand.
- Animal characters: gentle, playful, and recognisable through delivery.

Do not force multiple voices when doing so would make narration unnatural or inconsistent.

## 10.3 Audio Generation Strategy

Narration should be prepared in advance for published stories.

Recommended workflow:

1. An administrator adds the story script.
2. The administrator selects the narration language.
3. The system generates narration audio using a configured text-to-speech provider.
4. The administrator previews the result.
5. The administrator approves the audio.
6. The approved audio is stored.
7. The story is published with the correct audio reference.

Regenerate audio only when necessary.

Do not generate a new narration request every time a child presses Play.

## 10.4 Audio Metadata

Store:
- Story ID.
- Language.
- Voice identifier.
- Audio file location.
- Duration.
- Generation status.
- Approval status.
- Creation timestamp.
- Version.

The system must not confuse different languages or versions of a story.

# 11. Language Support

## 11.1 Initial Languages

The MVP should support:
- Telugu.
- English.
- Hindi.

Structure the content system to support Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali, and other languages in future releases.

## 11.2 Language Behaviour

Language availability must be managed per story.

For example, if a story has Telugu and English narration but no Hindi narration, the interface should display only the available options.

When the child changes languages:
- Load the matching script.
- Load the matching narration.
- Load language-specific subtitles when available.
- Update the interface language if the application supports that preference.

Do not simply translate the title while playing narration in a different language.

## 11.3 Translation Management

An administrator must be able to:
- Add a translation.
- Edit the translated script.
- Mark a translation as reviewed.
- Attach its narration audio.
- Publish the language version independently.

Translations must preserve the original meaning and cultural context.

# 12. Illustrated Storytelling and Video Support

## 12.1 MVP Presentation Mode

The application must support narrated illustrated stories.

Where full video is unavailable, display a sequence of story illustrations accompanied by narration.

Each scene should have:
- Illustration.
- Narration segment or timing reference, where supported.
- Optional caption.
- Scene order.

Illustrations must relate to the story and should not be unrelated decorative images.

## 12.2 Optional Animation

The data model must support a completed animated video associated with a story and language.

If a video is available:
- Show the Watch Story option.
- Play the actual video.
- Provide playback controls.
- Display subtitles when available.

If no video is available:
- Offer the illustrated narration experience.

Clearly distinguish between a fully animated video and an illustrated slideshow.

## 12.3 Video Generation

Full AI animation is not a mandatory MVP dependency.

When implemented, video production should be an administrator-managed background process.

The process may include:
1. Preparing a scene plan from the approved script.
2. Generating or selecting approved illustrations.
3. Generating animation clips through a configured provider.
4. Adding narration and sound.
5. Adding subtitles.
6. Combining scenes into a playable video.
7. Reviewing and publishing the result.

Do not claim that a video has been generated until a valid video file exists.

If the animation provider is unavailable, the story must remain accessible through its available audio and illustrations.

Inspect the existing project before selecting or replacing frameworks.

Keep the application modular.

Separate:
- Frontend components.
- Backend API routes.
- Authentication and authorisation.
- Story and category services.
- Media storage.

## 17.3 Media Storage

Store large audio, image, and video files in suitable persistent object storage.

The database should store their references and associated metadata.

Use access-controlled URLs or appropriate signed URLs for private media.

Published children's stories may use public or controlled delivery according to the chosen deployment model.

## 17.4 External Providers

Keep external services configurable.

Potential integrations include:
- A text-to-speech service for expressive narration.
- An optional image-generation provider.
- An optional AI video-generation provider.
- A media-rendering process for combining illustrations, audio, and subtitles.

Do not hardcode provider-specific logic throughout the application.

All credentials must be stored in server-side environment secrets.

Do not expose provider API keys in browser code, client responses, or logs.

## 17.5 Background Processing

Long-running audio or video generation must not block normal user requests.

Use an appropriate background-job mechanism for configured media-generation workflows.

Track:
- Pending.
- Processing.
- Completed.
- Failed.

Provide retry behaviour and meaningful error messages.

The children's app should continue serving already published stories if a provider is unavailable.

# 19. Security, Privacy, and Child Safety

The product is intended for children, so safety and privacy are essential.

Requirements:
- Collect the minimum personal information necessary.
- Secure administrative access.
- Validate uploads.
- Restrict allowed file types and sizes.
- Protect against common web vulnerabilities.
- Store secrets securely.
- Avoid exposing internal errors to users.
- Keep external links away from the primary children's interface.
- Review scripts and generated media before publication.
- Use age-appropriate language and visuals.
- Adapt frightening traditional stories for younger audiences.
- Respect cultural and regional differences.
- Use properly licensed or original content.
- Provide privacy-conscious defaults.

Where the application is made available to children, assess the privacy and parental-consent requirements applicable to the intended jurisdictions before launch.

Do not implement unnecessary targeted advertising or invasive tracking.

# 20. Performance and Reliability

The app must remain usable on common mobile devices and typical network connections.

Requirements:
- Optimise illustrations.
- Lazy-load nonessential images.
- Use efficient media delivery.
- Avoid unnecessary API requests.
- Load story metadata before large media when appropriate.
- Show a loading state while media is retrieved.
- Retry transient network errors where safe.
- Provide a fallback when optional video content is unavailable.
- Keep browsing functional when narration or video services fail.
- Avoid re-downloading media unnecessarily.
- Ensure database queries are efficient.

Pre-generated narration should be the default for published stories to reduce waiting time and external API usage.

Do not introduce invasive child profiling.

Any optional product analytics must be privacy-conscious and appropriate for the intended audience.

---

# 23. Error Handling and Empty States

Provide friendly messages for common situations.

Examples:

**No stories in a category:**

"More magical stories are coming soon!"

**Narration unavailable:**

"Oops! This story's voice is resting. You can read the story while we fix it."

**Network problem:**

"We couldn't load your story. Please try again."

**No favourites:**

"Your favourite stories will appear here. Tap the little heart on a story you love!"

**Media processing failure in the admin dashboard:**

Show the actual job status, a safe error summary, and a retry option when supported.

Do not display fake success messages or pretend unavailable media is playable.

# 24. Acceptance Criteria

The MVP is considered complete only when the following requirements have been implemented and tested.

## Homepage
- The homepage displays the intended cute storybook design.
- Published categories and stories load from the database.
- Story cards open the correct story.
- Empty categories are handled correctly.
- Mobile layouts do not overflow horizontally.

## Story Library
- At least 30 complete, curated story entries are available.
- Categories and search work.
- Only published stories are visible to children.
- Story titles and descriptions match the selected story.
- Available languages are accurately displayed.

## Narration
- Selecting Listen plays the correct audio.
- Play, pause, resume, restart, and volume controls work.
- The correct language's audio is used.
- Missing audio produces a clear error.
- Narration is not unnecessarily regenerated on every playback.
- Playback progress is preserved where supported.

## Illustrated Stories and Videos
- Story illustrations correspond to the selected story.
- Available videos play correctly.
- Illustrated narration works when a completed video is unavailable.
- The application does not misrepresent a slideshow as a full animation.
- Broken media references are detected and handled.

## Favourites and Progress
- Favourites can be added and removed.
- Saved favourites appear in the correct section.
- Listening progress can be resumed when supported.
- Guest and authenticated storage behave according to the selected design.

## Admin Dashboard
- Administrators can add, edit, review, publish, and archive stories.
- Translations and media assets can be managed.
- Unauthorised users cannot access administrative operations.
- Published content is validated.
- Media job failures are visible to administrators.

## Safety and Quality
- Child-facing content is age-appropriate.
- No unnecessary personal information is collected.
- Secrets are not exposed in frontend code.
- Buttons and links work as intended.
- Loading, empty, and error states are implemented.
- The application works on desktop, tablet, and mobile.
